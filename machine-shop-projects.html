Machine Shop Projects

Map Transport Cart

Description

The Map Transport carts were contracted by the Johns Hopkins Milton S. Eisenhower Library to move the various maps they have in storage. There are 2 different versions of the cart, a mobile and stationary version. The mobile carts are used for transport on foot, and the stationary cart is placed in the back of a van for longer distance transport. The frames are constructed of 80/20 bars and hardware, and various other pieces of hardware such as latches and castor wheels are purchased from McMaster-Carr. The plastic profiles were outsourced from a plastics manufacturer.

Bioreactor Probe Holder

Description

Contracted job from the Shrestha Lab in Material Science at JHU. A mount is required to hold various probes in place (temperature, pH, pressure) while the material within the bioreactor is stirred to ensure accurate measurements. Test prints were made of PLA determine the ideal dimensions for the mount, and final versions of each iteration were made of PC-ABS. Versions 1-3 were designed to mount to the inside of the bottle, while versions 4-6 were designed to fit closer to the opening of the bottle.

Bioreactor Propeller

Description

Also contracted by the Shrestha Lab, this propeller had to fit within the bioreactor to stir the mixture inside together. The material could not be made of plastic due to corrosion. Instead, sheet metal steel was cut out using a waterjet, and the design was modeled in Solidworks.

Rolling Toolbox Clips

Description

Rolling Toolbox Clips is a project in-house to hold DeWalt Tool Cases to Rolling DeWalt Toolboxes for ease of transport when doing contracted work outside of the machine shop. Earlier iterations were printed out of ABS and PC-ABS with spring plungers from McMaster-Carr. The final iteration was printed out of PLA, and incorporate the toolbox toggle latches.

Vacuum Adapter

Description

The vacuum adapter was contracted from a Johns Hopkins Lab to connect a nozzle with a circular profile to a vacuum with a pentagon profile. The 3D model was 3D printed on a Stratasys F370 out of PC-ABS. Prototypes for test-fitting were printed on an Original Prusa MK4 with Prusament PLA.

Faraday Cages

Description

The Faraday Cage(s) is a project contracted by a lab at the Johns Hopkins Medical Campus, which is used to protect sensitive equipment from electric discharges. The frame is constructed using 80/20 bars and hardware, aluminum screen, and aluminum screen rails. Access holes for wiring are lined with a 3D printed wire guard. Assembly done in house.

Incubator Lab Trays

Description

Contracted job by a lab at the Johns Hopkins Medical Campus. Steel trays inside of an incubator collapsed after continued use. The failure points are located along the spot welds on the support beams due to the actual tray deforming under said load. The trays, supports, and mounting rails were modeled using the sheet metal tool in Solidworks. To reinforce the design, more supports were added underneath the tray, and the tray dimensions were increased slightly so that more contact is made with the mounting rails. The required number of added supports was determined using a FEA in Solidworks. Engineering drawings were produced for outsourcing.

Mosquito Boxes

Description

The mosquito boxes were contracted by a Johns Hopkins Lab to study mosquitoes. The boxes are made of bonded polycarbonate with a lid connected by plastic hinges. The polycarbonate is manufactured and bonded together by Laird Plastics. COTS parts are from McMaster-Carr. Engineering drawings were produced for outsourcing from Laird Plastics.

Rat Brain Surgery Mount

Description

This project was contracted by a Johns Hopkins Neuroscience lab on the medical campus. The device is a mount for a rat’s head which allow researchers to do surgery on their brains. The lab required more pieces so they could conduct more surgeries. The part is made of a titanium alloy, and I had to take dimensions of the original part, and model it to potentially outsource the part. Professional machinists in the shop opted to make the parts on a wire EDM.

Crucible Holder Modifications

Description

Internal project for the student shop. Tasked with modifying a crucible holder for the "Manufacturing Engineering" Lab course. Braces were designed and cut out of 1/4" steel on the waterjet. Once cut, the braces were MIG welded onto the sides of the crucible holder. Once welded, an opening was cut in the front of the crucible arms using an angle grinder
